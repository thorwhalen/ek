ek.qe.calibrate#

Calibration: make a raw score mean a probability (the non-optional stage).

A confidence of 0.9 should mean “correct 90% of the time.” Modern models violate this badly – they are systematically overconfident (Guo et al. 2017), and RLHF makes LLMs worse. So **calibration is non-optional: never gate a raw posterior or a raw logprob** (misc/docs/ek_03 §2, Hard Rule 1). A Calibrator is fit on a labelled holdout of (raw_score, field_correct?) pairs and maps any later raw score to a calibrated probability.

Three methods, by what input you have:

  • PlattCalibrator – logistic fit on any scalar score (no logits needed): the default for aggregated OCR confidence or aggregated logprobs.

  • IsotonicCalibrator – non-parametric monotonic fit; more flexible, needs more data, can overfit small sets.

  • TemperatureCalibrator – a single scalar T on logits; use only when you have logits (it does not change the argmax).

All three are pure-Python (stdlib only) so the calibration stage works with zero extra dependencies; sklearn_calibrator() / netcal_calibrator() offer the library-backed equivalents behind the ek[calibration] extra. Measure calibration with expected_calibration_error() (+ a reliability curve). For per-field-type validity, wrap per group with GroupCalibrator (Mondrian / class-conditional) – distribution-free conditional coverage is otherwise impossible (Hard Rule 2). When the raw signals are heterogeneous (a judge score next to a logprob next to a ROVER agreement), calibrate each signal family on its own with SignalFamilyCalibrator and fuse the calibrated probabilities with a documented combiner (mean_probability() by default, min_probability() for a conservative gate), rather than calibrating the mean of incomparable scores.

Calibrate at the granularity of the decision (gate on fields -> calibrate a “field-correct?” target), and persist the fit (save_calibrator()); calibration is dataset-specific and decays, so re-fit on drift.

Example

>>> # An overconfident raw signal, calibrated against ground truth.
>>> raw =     [0.95, 0.93, 0.92, 0.90, 0.55, 0.52, 0.51, 0.50]
>>> correct = [True, True, False, False, True, False, False, False]
>>> cal = PlattCalibrator().fit(raw, correct)
>>> cal(0.95) < 0.95            # overconfidence pulled down
True
>>> 0.0 <= cal(0.5) <= 1.0
True

Per-group (Mondrian) calibration: one calibrator per NodeType/FieldSpec.

Distribution-free conditional (per-field-type) coverage is impossible in general (Barber et al. 2019); calibrating separately per group restores it approximately. fit takes a parallel groups sequence; __call__ routes by group key, falling back to a pooled calibrator for unseen groups.

class ek.qe.calibrate.SignalFamilyCalibrator(calibrators=dict(), combine=<function mean_probability>, factory=<class 'ek.qe.calibrate.PlattCalibrator'>, fusion=None, kind='signal_family')[source]#

Bases: object

One calibrator per signal family, fused by a documented combination rule.

Averaging heterogeneous raw signals (an LLM-judge score, a token logprob, a ROVER agreement) and calibrating the mean discards exactly what a calibrator needs: each family has its own scale, reliability and direction of miscalibration. This calibrates every signal by name with its own calibrator, then fuses the calibrated probabilities with combine.

Pass it (or simply a Mapping[str, Calibrator]) as estimate_quality(..., calibrator=...).

The fused value must itself be calibrated before it is gated. Fusing calibrated probabilities (by mean, min, …) does not yield a calibrated probability: agreeing families make the mean under-confident, a weak family drags it toward the base rate. So fit() also learns a second-stage fusion calibrator on combine’s output, and __call__ applies it. Without a fusion calibrator (e.g. a hand-built mapping), ek.estimate_quality() refuses to gate a value fused from two or more families unless told assume_calibrated=True (Hard Rule 1). A value from a single family is that family’s calibrated probability and gates as is.

A field’s own upstream confidence is just one more family here, named "confidence": include it in the training rows under that name.

Parameters:

  • calibrators (dict) – signal name -> Calibrator. A "*" entry is the fallback for any signal without its own entry. A signal with neither is refused (ValueError) rather than silently dropped or passed through raw (Hard Rule 1).

  • combine (Callable[[Mapping[str, float]], float]) – Mapping[str, float] -> float fusing the calibrated values; mean_probability() (default) or min_probability(), or any callable of that shape (e.g. a learned stacker).

  • factory (Callable[[], Any]) – Zero-arg callable producing a fresh calibrator, used by fit().

  • fusion (Any) – Second-stage calibrator applied to combine’s output (learned by fit()); None leaves the fused value uncalibrated.

Example

>>> rows = [{"judge": j, "logprob": l} for j, l in
...         [(0.9, -0.1), (0.8, -0.2), (0.85, -2.0), (0.3, -0.1), (0.2, -2.5), (0.7, -1.8)]]
>>> correct = [True, True, False, True, False, False]
>>> cal = SignalFamilyCalibrator().fit(rows, correct)
>>> sorted(cal.calibrators)
['judge', 'logprob']
>>> 0.0 <= cal({"judge": 0.9, "logprob": -0.1}) <= 1.0
True

ek.qe.calibrate.expected_calibration_error(probs, correct, *, n_bins=10)[source]#

Expected Calibration Error: weighted mean gap between confidence and accuracy.

Bins predictions by confidence into n_bins equal-width bins and averages |mean_confidence - accuracy| weighted by bin population. 0 is perfect. Non-finite probs are skipped; out-of-range probs are clamped into [0, 1].
